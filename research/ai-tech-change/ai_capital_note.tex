\begin{abstract}
\noindent This note compares how output, the real interest rate, the real wage and the
labor share respond to technical progress in AI capital and in regular capital.
Regular capital and labor form a Cobb-Douglas bundle, and AI capital substitutes for
that bundle ($\e=1.5$). Progress is either disembodied (in the production function) or
embodied (a lower price of new capital in the accumulation equation). Both capital
stocks face the same moderate adjustment costs. Three timing patterns are compared: a
surprise permanent jump; an S-curve centered on year 10 and known at year 0; and a mix
in which half the improvement arrives as a jump and half along the S-curve.
Elasticities are the same throughout, and every shock raises long-run output by the
same 10 percent. In the long run, which is in closed form, AI progress raises the wage by 7.4
percent and lowers the labor share by 1.8 points. Regular-capital progress raises the
wage by the full 10 percent and leaves the labor share unchanged. In the short run, the
interest rate rises most for embodied AI progress after a jump, and most for
disembodied regular-capital progress along an S-curve. Output and wage gains arrive
fastest under disembodied progress. Output converges faster for AI than for regular
capital, but AI wages lag AI output. The mixed case lies close to the average of the
two pure cases.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model}

Notation follows \citet{kiley2000}. Production is
\begin{equation*}
X=(\Gamma_KK)^a(ZN)^{1-a},\qquad
Y=\Big[\omega^{1/\e}X^{\frac{\e-1}{\e}}+(1-\omega)^{1/\e}(\Gamma_AA)^{\frac{\e-1}{\e}}\Big]^{\frac{\e}{\e-1}},\qquad \e=1.5 ,
\end{equation*}
so AI capital $A$ substitutes for both labor and regular capital $K$. Capital is
measured in efficiency units and accumulates as $K_j'=(1-d_j)K_j+I_j/P_j$, where $P_j$ is the
output price of a new efficiency unit. Adjustment costs, following \citet{kiley2004}, are
\begin{equation*}
P_j\,\tfrac{c_j}{2}\,(i_j-x_j)^2K_j,\qquad i_j=\frac{I_j}{P_jK_j},\qquad c_j=\frac1{x_j}.
\end{equation*}
They vanish at the balanced-growth investment rate $x_j=g+d_j$, and they give both stocks the same
unit elasticity of investment with respect to $Q_j/P_j$ (the paper's ``moderate'' case).
\emph{Disembodied} progress raises $\Gamma_j$, which improves the installed stock.
\emph{Embodied} progress lowers $P_j$, which improves only new investment. Utility is
logarithmic and hours are fixed.

\paragraph{Calibration.} $a=0.282$, $d_K=0.066$, $d_A=0.20$, trend growth $g=1.5$ percent,
initial $r=7.5$ percent, initial AI income share 3 percent, initial labor share 69.6 percent.

\begin{proposition}[Balanced growth path]\label{prop:bgp}
With constant technology levels, $Q_j=P_j$, $1+r=(1+g)/B$ and $u_j=r+d_j$. Let
$\lambda_j=P_ju_j/\Gamma_j$ be the cost of an effective unit of capital $j$. Then
\begin{gather*}
s_A=(1-\omega)\lambda_A^{1-\e},\qquad \text{labor share}=(1-a)(1-s_A),\qquad
P_X=\Big(\frac{1-s_A}{\omega}\Big)^{\frac{1}{1-\e}},\\
\ln\frac WZ=\frac{\ln P_X}{1-a}-\frac{a}{1-a}\ln\lambda_K+\text{const},\qquad
\ln\frac YZ=\ln\frac WZ-\ln(1-s_A)+\text{const}.
\end{gather*}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Long run, same output gain $\gamma_Y$]\label{prop:lr}
Disembodied and embodied progress have the same long-run effects ($\ln\Gamma_j=-\ln P_j$),
and the interest rate returns to its initial level.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Regular capital}: $\ln(\Gamma_K/P_K)=\frac{1-a}{a}\gamma_Y$, $\Delta\ln W=\gamma_Y$, and the labor share is unchanged.
\item \emph{AI capital}: $\Delta\ln W=\dfrac{\gamma_Y}{1+(1-a)(\e-1)}$ and
$\Delta\ln(\text{labor share})=-\dfrac{(1-a)(\e-1)}{1+(1-a)(\e-1)}\gamma_Y$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\emph{Proof.} By Proposition~\ref{prop:bgp},
$\ln(Y/Z)=\big[\frac{1}{(1-a)(1-\e)}-1\big]\ln(1-s_A)-\frac{a}{1-a}\ln\lambda_K+$const, and
$\ln W=\ln Y+\ln(1-s_A)$. Regular-capital progress moves only $\lambda_K$, and AI progress
moves only $s_A$. \qed

For $\gamma_Y=10$ percent, regular-capital technology rises 29 percent, against 3.4-fold
for AI, because AI starts small. AI's income share rises from 3.0 to 5.5 percent. In the long
run, AI progress raises the wage 7.4 percent and lowers the labor share from 69.6 to
67.8 percent. Regular-capital progress raises the wage 10 percent and leaves the labor share unchanged.

\section{Results}

All experiments start on the initial balanced growth path. In the \emph{jump},
technology rises permanently and unexpectedly at $t=0$. In the \emph{S-curve}, a logistic
rise centered on year 10 is announced at $t=0$. In the \emph{half/half} case, half the
log improvement arrives as a jump at $t=0$ and the other half along the same S-curve,
also announced at $t=0$. Each is solved as a nonlinear perfect-foresight problem.
Output (net of adjustment costs) and wages are log deviations from the pre-shock path,
and the labor share is relative to gross output.

\subsection*{Output}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Jump.} Disembodied progress raises output on impact, by 6.9 percent for regular capital and 4.1 for AI.
Embodied progress cannot raise output until new capital is installed, and net output
dips on impact (by 0.4 percent for regular capital and 1.5 for AI) because the
investment boom incurs adjustment costs. AI output then catches up quickly. AI capital
depreciates fast and has low adjustment costs per unit of investment, so after 10 years
output is up 7.8 (AI, disembodied) and 6.6 (AI, embodied), against 8.3 and 4.3 for
regular capital.
\item \emph{S-curve.} Output tracks the innovation. At year 20 it is up 8.1 (regular,
disembodied), 7.8 (AI, disembodied), 5.8 (AI, embodied) and 3.8 percent (regular,
embodied). Embodied regular-capital progress is the slowest to raise output in every timing pattern.
\item \emph{Half/half.} Output jumps by about half the pure-jump amount and then follows the S-curve.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Real interest rate}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Jump.} The rate jumps at once and then declines. On impact the ranking is
AI embodied (8.7 percent), regular embodied (8.1), AI disembodied (8.05) and regular
disembodied (7.7). Embodied progress raises $r$ more because the output gain requires an
investment boom first. AI raises $r$ more than
regular capital because a larger share of its long-run gain must be accumulated.
Disembodied AI delivers 3.0 of its 7.4 percent wage gain on impact, against 7.0 of 10
percent for regular capital. Over 40 years,
the excess return adds up to about 9 percentage points in both embodied cases, against 4.7 for disembodied AI and 2.7
for disembodied regular capital.
\item \emph{S-curve.} The rate barely moves at announcement. Under disembodied progress it
rises ahead of the innovation and peaks with it (year 10--11): 8.3 percent for regular
capital and 8.15 for AI. Under embodied progress it peaks later (years 15--16) and lower,
at 7.9 for regular capital and 8.0 for AI, but the response lasts longer. The 40-year
cumulative effects are similar, 7.4--9.4 percentage points.
\item \emph{Half/half.} The rate has two humps, an impact response from the jump and a
second peak as the S-curve arrives (years 10--14). For disembodied progress the second
peak is larger (7.97 regular, 7.94 AI). For embodied AI the impact response is larger (8.00).
Each path is close to the average of the pure jump and pure S-curve paths. For example,
$r_1=7.63$ for disembodied regular capital, against an average of 7.63.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Real wage}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Long run}: 10.0 percent for regular capital and 7.4 percent for AI (Proposition~\ref{prop:lr}).
\item \emph{Jump.} Disembodied progress raises the wage on impact, by 7.0 percent for
regular capital and 3.0 for AI. Embodied progress starts from zero. By year 10 the
wage is up 8.4 (regular, disembodied), 5.5 (AI, disembodied), 4.5 (AI, embodied) and
4.4 percent (regular, embodied).
\item \emph{S-curve.} The wage follows the innovation, and much more slowly when progress is
embodied. At year 10 it is up 3.7 percent (regular, disembodied), 2.1 (AI, disembodied), 0.8 (AI,
embodied) and 0.6 (regular, embodied). At year 20 the figures are 8.1, 5.4, 4.1 and 4.0.
\item \emph{Half/half.} Wages jump by half the disembodied pure-jump amount (3.5 regular,
1.4 AI). By year 10 they are up 6.0 (regular, disembodied), 3.6 (AI, disembodied), 2.5 (AI,
embodied) and 2.5 percent (regular, embodied).
\item \emph{AI wages lag AI output.} Because the AI share is rising, wages grow more slowly than
output under AI progress. At year 10 of the jump, AI output is up 7.8 percent but wages only
5.5 percent. Under regular-capital progress the two move together.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Labor share}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Regular capital}: essentially unchanged throughout, within 0.05 points.
\item \emph{AI}: falls 1.8 points to 67.8 percent. After a disembodied jump, about 60 percent of
the fall happens on impact. After an embodied jump it happens as AI capital is installed
(68.2 percent at year 10). Along the S-curve it tracks the innovation, at 68.9 percent
(disembodied) or 69.4 percent (embodied) in year 10. In the half/half case, half
the disembodied fall happens on impact (to 69.2 percent).
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary}
With the same elasticities and the same long-run output gain:
\begin{enumerate}
\item AI progress delivers about three-quarters of the wage gain of regular-capital
progress and lowers the labor share. Regular-capital progress leaves the labor share
unchanged. These long-run results are in closed form and do not depend on whether
progress is embodied or disembodied.
\item Output gains arrive fastest under disembodied progress. Under embodied AI progress
they arrive faster than under embodied regular-capital progress, because AI capital
turns over quickly. Embodied jumps briefly lower net output while the investment boom
absorbs resources.
\item The interest rate rises temporarily in every case. A surprise jump raises it most
when progress is embodied and in AI. An anticipated S-curve raises it most, and soonest,
when progress is disembodied. Embodied progress produces a later, smaller but more
persistent rise.
\item AI wages lag AI output, and embodied progress delays wage gains by roughly five to ten
years compared with disembodied progress.
\item A mix of a jump and an S-curve gives approximately the average of the two pure
responses, so the timing patterns can be combined roughly linearly.
\end{enumerate}
